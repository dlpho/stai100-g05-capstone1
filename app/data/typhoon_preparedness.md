# Typhoon Preparedness Guidelines for Filipino Farmers

Typhoons present a significant risk to agriculture in the Philippines. Proper planning can mitigate crop loss, protect livestock, and ensure farm safety.

## Before the Typhoon
* **Harvest Mature Crops**: Immediately harvest crops that are close to maturity (such as rice, corn, or vegetables) to secure whatever yield is possible before the storm hits.
* **Clear Drainage Canals**: Clean and deepen farm ditches, canals, and drainage pathways to allow floodwaters to recede quickly.
* **Secure Farm Structures**: Reinforce greenhouses, poultry houses, and animal sheds. Tie down loose equipment and move sensitive farm machinery to higher ground.
* **Protect Livestock**: Evacuate animals to high-elevation, covered shelters. Store adequate livestock feed and clean water in elevated, dry places.
* **Prune Large Trees**: Trim branches of large trees near structures and crop beds to prevent them from snapping and causing physical damage.

## During the Typhoon
* **Ensure Personal Safety**: Do not attempt to work in fields or rescue livestock during active typhoon conditions. Stay indoors or in designated evacuation centers.
* **Monitor Flood Levels**: Watch official PAGASA updates and local announcements on rising water levels.

## After the Typhoon
* **Drain Excess Water**: Inspect fields immediately and drain logged water as quickly as possible, especially from root crops and sensitive vegetables.
* **Apply Quick-acting Fertilizers**: For partially damaged crops, apply quick-acting foliar fertilizers to boost recovery once weather clears.
* **Inspect for Pests and Disease**: Warm, wet conditions post-typhoon are ideal for bacterial blight, blast, and pest infestations. Apply organic or target treatments immediately.
* **Report Damage**: Document and report crop damages to the local municipal agriculture office (DA-MAO) for potential government rehabilitation support.

# Drought and El Niño Mitigation Strategies for Filipino Farmers

El Niño and prolonged dry periods dry up water sources and severely affect agricultural yields in the Philippines. Implementing adaptive water management and farming practices is vital.

## Water Conservation & Management
* **Rainwater Harvesting**: Construct small farm reservoirs (SFRs) or farm ponds during the wet season to collect runoff water for dry season use.
* **Drip Irrigation**: Install drip irrigation systems to deliver water directly to crop roots, reducing evaporation and water waste.
* **Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)**: For rice farmers, implement AWD technology to reduce water usage by up to 30% without reducing yields. Alternate flooding the field and letting it dry naturally before re-flooding.

## Crop Planning & Practices
* **Cultivate Drought-Tolerant Varieties**: Switch to climate-resilient, early-maturing, or drought-tolerant crop varieties (e.g., specific NSIC certified rice and corn seeds).
* **Crop Diversification**: Plant heat-tolerant crops like cassava, sweet potato (kamote), mungbeans (mungo), and vegetables that require less water than rice during dry periods.
* **Mulching**: Apply organic mulch (rice straw, dried leaves, coconut coir) around crops to conserve soil moisture and lower soil temperature.

## Soil & Farm Management
* **Increase Soil Organic Matter**: Incorporate compost and manure to improve the soil's water-retention capacity.
* **Adjust Planting Calendar**: Shift planting calendars according to PAGASA seasonal weather outlooks to avoid crop growth stages coinciding with peak dry months.
* **Pest and Disease Vigilance**: Keep fields clean. Higher temperatures promote the rapid reproduction of sucking insects (e.g., thrips, aphids, spider mites) which spread viral diseases.
